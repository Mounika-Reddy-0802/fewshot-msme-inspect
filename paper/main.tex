\begin{abstract}
Small manufacturers need visual inspection that starts from a handful of photos
of good parts, runs on hardware they already own, and can take on a new product
without disturbing the ones in production. We benchmark a training-free
detector for this setting: a frozen OpenCLIP backbone, an append-only per-class
memory of patch tokens, and optional fusion with text prompts. On MVTec AD and
VisA, three backbones (ViT-B/32, B/16, L/14), two precisions (FP32, INT8) and
1/5/10 shots over three seeds, we report accuracy and CPU cost together. With
ten good photos, ViT-L/14 reaches 0.933 image and 0.945 pixel AUROC on MVTec AD;
ViT-B/16 with multi-scale windows and text fusion reaches 0.925 at a
single-pass cost. The cheaper ViT-B/32 is statistically indistinguishable from
B/16 on MVTec AD but loses 0.05--0.09 AUROC on VisA, whose defects are smaller
than one of its patches. INT8 costs 0.005--0.02 AUROC and never changes which
backbone to choose. Adding a product is bit-identical to building it in from
the start and leaves every existing product's scores unchanged. Inspecting
10,000 parts a day costs under \textrm{Rs}~41 of electricity a year.

\section{Method}

\paragraph{Encoder.} A frozen OpenCLIP ViT (OpenAI weights, QuickGELU
activation) maps a $224\times224$ image to a pooled embedding in the 512-dim
image--text space and to $H\times W$ patch tokens. We keep patch tokens at the
transformer width (768 for B/32 and B/16, 1024 for L/14) rather than projecting
them to 512: the unprojected tokens are $+0.020$ to $+0.026$ image AUROC better
(3.7--4.4$\times$ the paired standard error, 15 categories $\times$ 3 seeds;
\texttt{dim\_ablation.json}). No parameter is trained anywhere.

\paragraph{Append-only memory.} Adding a product stores the $\ell_2$-normalised
patch tokens of its $k$ good photos in a bank owned by that product alone. A
query patch's anomaly is $1-\max_j \cos(q, m_j)$ over the bank; the image score
is the maximum over patches, and the $H\times W$ map is bilinearly upsampled and
Gaussian-smoothed ($\sigma=4$). Because banks share nothing, adding a product
cannot change any other product's scores; we verify this with exact tensor
equality rather than assume it (Section~\ref{sec:incremental}).

\paragraph{Multi-scale windows.} Following WinCLIP's observation that defects
are judged better over a neighbourhood~\cite{jeong2023winclip}, but without its
per-window encoder passes, we average-pool the already computed patch tokens over
$2\times2$ and $3\times3$ windows, score each scale against an equally pooled
bank, spread window scores back over the patches they cover, and average the
scales. Scale 1 alone is exactly the memory above.

\paragraph{Text fusion.} Unprojected patch tokens cannot be compared with text,
but the pooled embedding can. The image score becomes
$(1-w)\,s_\text{memory} + w\,s_\text{text}$, where $s_\text{text}$ is the
softmax weight of a ``defective $\langle$object$\rangle$'' prompt ensemble
against a ``normal'' one; a typed defect description is added to the defective
ensemble. The map is unaffected. We fix $w=0.5$, the equal weighting used by
WinCLIP+, and report the full grid $w\in\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,1\}$ rather than
selecting on test data.

\paragraph{Deployment.} The visual tower is exported to ONNX (outputs identical
to PyTorch: patch cosine 1.00000 and identical anomaly scores on 50 real test
images; \texttt{fidelity\_probe.json}) and dynamically quantised to INT8, which
needs no calibration set.

\section{Protocol}
Datasets: MVTec AD~\cite{bergmann2019mvtec} (15 categories) and
VisA~\cite{zou2022visa} (12). Splits: 1/5/10 normal support images, nested
($1\subset5\subset10$) so extra shots are measured against the same starting
images, on a fixed test set, for three seeds (42, 1, 2); manifests are
committed. Metrics: image and pixel AUROC. Comparisons are paired over identical
(category, shots, seed) splits and reported as the mean delta and its multiple of
the paired standard error (SE); we call an effect established above $2\times$SE.
Hardware: one Intel i7-13700H laptop (6 performance + 8 efficiency cores), CPU
only; latency is the median of 20 batch-size-1 passes after 5 warm-up passes at
6 threads (Section~\ref{sec:cost} explains why not 14). \todo{NEU-DET if completed.}

\paragraph{Backbone choice depends on defect size.} On MVTec AD no B/16--B/32
difference reaches $2\times$SE (at 10 shots: $+0.000$ image, $-0.004$ pixel). On
VisA, B/16 wins at every shot count ($+0.093$, $+0.050$, $+0.052$ image;
4.4--5.5$\times$SE). Measured from the masks, a VisA defect covers 0.04--0.9\% of
the image against 4.6\% on MVTec AD; a B/32 patch covers 2.0\%, a B/16 patch
0.5\%. B/16's largest VisA gains are the smallest-defect categories. L/14
(0.3\% per patch) improves on B/16 again: $+0.030$ image on MVTec AD
(4.6$\times$SE), largest on \texttt{toothbrush} ($+0.117$) and \texttt{screw}
($+0.086$); on VisA its pixel gain is established ($+0.013$, 3.9$\times$SE) but
its image gain is not ($+0.012$, 1.7$\times$SE).

\paragraph{More shots buy predictability.} On MVTec AD with B/16 the mean
seed-to-seed standard deviation of image AUROC falls from 0.027 at one shot to
0.012 at ten; single-seed few-shot numbers understate this variance.

\paragraph{INT8 costs little and changes nothing structural.} Dynamic INT8 makes
every graph $4\times$ smaller and B/32 1.3$\times$ / B/16 1.8$\times$ faster.
It costs 0.005--0.02 image AUROC across both datasets and backbones. Which
backbone loses more is dataset-dependent (B/16 on MVTec AD, neither clearly on
VisA), and in no case does quantisation change which backbone should be chosen.

\subsection{Adding a product}\label{sec:incremental}
Twelve MVTec categories form a deployed memory; three held-out categories
(fixed in advance as the alphabetically last) are appended from 5 or 10 photos.
For every seed and shot count: (i) the twelve existing products score
\emph{bit-identically} before and after; (ii) each appended product scores
bit-identically to a memory built with all fifteen from the start
(\texttt{incremental\_holdout\_*.json}). Appending costs \todo{idle-machine
end-to-end seconds} and 3.0\,MB (B/16, 5 shots); sequentially adding all fifteen
products takes 1.9\,ms of memory work each (\texttt{incremental\_5shot.json}).

\section{Limitations}
Datasets are public benchmarks, not a shop floor. Thin defects remain below the
patch resolution (\texttt{screw}: 0.792 at 10 shots even with L/14). Image scores
still confuse tiny defects with normal variation on VisA (0.855 best) while maps
localise well (pixel $\ge$0.95). We did not reproduce WinCLIP or AnomalyCLIP, nor
test static INT8. \todo{NEU-DET, MPDD.}
